## Firmware

- Touch Trezor's files as little as possible: new files over edits, `#ifdef`
  switches beside upstream code, never reformat or move it. Each sync replays our
  diff; every upstream line we change is a conflict candidate.
- Move to a newer upstream only with `firmware/sync-upstream.sh`; never copy
  upstream files over by hand or edit the first line of `vendor-pins.txt`. The
  sync replays committed files only: commit firmware changes first.
- Build with `firmware/docker-build.sh` (host has no ARM toolchain). Both it and
  the sync resolve physical paths: this checkout is reachable as
  `~/linux/ivo/storage/...` and `~/storage/...`, and git reports the latter.
- The upstream T3T2 emulator did not build at `5fdc180` (devkit too): no UI
  preview until Trezor fixes it.
- Bring-up, flashing and crash analysis over SWD: `firmware/swd.py` (info, trustzone,
  flash, state, gdb). On a bootloader red screen the error is in bootargs RAM, and
  `state` decodes it: the display need not work.

## The board file belongs to the owner

- **Never write `board/wallet.kicad_pcb` while KiCad may have it open**; a reload
  drops their unsaved routing. Commit their file as-is first, work on a copy, and
  install only if the original is unchanged (md5) — or ask / wait for "closing kicad".
- `scripts/newBoard.py --force` destroys hand work. After the first hand edit, only
  `syncBoard.py` (dry run first; `--apply` writes).
- On a hand-worked board make targeted fixes only. Global re-placement or
  crossing-quench made routability worse every time it was tried.

## Owner's copper and placement rules

- No stale copper: no stubs, no plane vias that reach no pad. `cleanCopper.py BOARD --check`
  must pass before every commit.
- Power fanouts thick (up to 0.4 mm, capped at the pad's narrow side), never a thin neck
  on a plane via (`widenFanouts.py`).
- In1 = V3V3 plane, In2 = solid GND (parts are on B.Cu and reference In2). One via per
  3.3 V pad, short fanout; never 3.3 V trunks or daisy chains between plane vias.
- No GND pad-to-via ties — the outer pour plus stitching vias carry GND. Kept ties are
  listed in `scripts/stripGndTies.py` (switching stages, exposed pads carrying heat,
  RF ground, ESD clamp, the owner's C_USBSW/R_FB2 tie). Stitching vias are "free" vias,
  or KiCad's interactive router shoves and re-nets them.
- The owner does tie a pad to a GND via on purpose where the pour would reach it only by
  fragmenting (2026-10-25: the charger's gate FET and ISET/VSET/input-cap pads, the radio
  UART translator, the SE050's ground pins). A tie DRC calls redundant is not therefore
  clutter: one removed in a quality pass came straight back. Add each new owner tie to
  `stripGndTies.py`'s KEEP, which `routeDefects.py` also skips.
- Route differential pairs and other critical nets **before** general signals
  (autorouted charger lines once took the USB pair's only corridor).
- Accept autorouted nets one at a time: near-shortest length, no detour, hairpin or
  extra via. Otherwise rip and leave them to the owner. The owner judges by look: a
  DRC-clean full autoroute was still rejected-grade.
- Placement: courtyards may touch (owner's median gap 0.01 mm); decaps hug their pins;
  rotate an IC so its pins face their partner, judged with its passives re-seated;
  pull-ups and series parts inline between source and destination. Judge routability
  with a trial route (`trialRoute.sh` / `routeGauge.py`), never with spacing or
  crossing proxies — those disagreed with the router.
- From the owner's hand pass on the chin (2026-10-23), checked by `placementScan.py`
  and `checkFpc.py` — run both after any placement:
  - A decap sits at its **chip's** rail pin, never a connector's (the USB buck's input
    cap had landed on USB-C's VBUS pin, on the other face).
  - A passive goes on the **face of the part it serves**: a net whose every other pad
    is on the other face costs a via. The joystick's ladder belongs on the front, in a
    row between the joystick and the cell, not on the back under it.
  - The front under the cell's flanks is usable for low parts: the cell's underside
    rises from 0.25 mm on its axis; `checkFpc.py` wants 0.3 mm over each part.
  - A **test pad sits beside its pin**, on the near side of any no-via zone and any
    run that could wall it off: the radio's SWD pads out over the module (no vias
    under it) were walled off by JOY_A and routed at 2–2.5×; beside the module's SWD
    pins they route in 3 mm.
  - Every drilled hole blocks both faces: the placer once put a front resistor over
    USB-C's peg (its same-side checks did not see a back part's legs; fixed).
  - **Pins face the way their route leaves.** The USB switch's MCU-side pins faced the
    TFT slot 0.45 mm away, so its diff pair could not even start; turned so they face
    west, toward the pair's bridge, with the connector-side pins facing the ESD clamp.
    And no free stitching via in a pair's exit (one blocked the switch's).
  - **A small-signal node is one cluster** (third pass): the boost's feedback divider
    and feed-forward cap within 1.3-1.9 mm of each other, the cell-sense filter cap
    beside its resistor; an output cap stays with its regulator's output on the same face
    (not across the board on the load's pin); a pull-up sits at its rail's source pin.
    `placementScan.py` lists nodes whose passives step more than 2 mm apart; spreading
    parts apart for room (`nudgeApart.py`) is what pulled the feedback node open.
  - **How the owner closed the fourth pass** (2026-10-23): the charger corner's DRC
    conflicts went by re-seating and turning its passives (the ISET/VSET/TMR resistors,
    the input cap), not by rerouting; the reverse-FET gate network stayed split and its
    V_BOOST tap (microamps) runs at 0.15-0.2 mm beside REV_G along the south edge: a
    rail's class width is for its current path, not for every tap; the air-gap switch's
    boxed-in shield tabs got a short tie to a via each (kept in `stripGndTies.py`).
  - The router's own power/ground habits were slop to the owner: 1-2 mm "slabs"
    (its `fatten` pass) and GND tracks tying pads the pour missed. Both are opt-in knobs
    now (`fatten`, `gndRepair=join`) on the router's `under-part-jumps` branch; leave
    them off.
  - **Re-placing a region** (the cell to the back, 2026-10-25): seed every displaced part
    deliberately (block by block, from pin maps), legalize with optimizePlacement's
    rules, then judge each idea by a trial route (routeGauge.py), one change at a time.
    Greedy block placement from a poor seed and KiCadRoutingTools' quench
    (`scripts/krtPlace.sh`, side-blind, wrapped per face) both scored worse than a
    deliberate seed. What paid: the USB switch and ESD clamp beside USB-C (−75 mm,
    −10 vias), the charger beside the middle bridge it feeds the cell through (−78 mm).
  - **Never rip whole nets after moving parts** (owner, 2026-10-25: "you've set me back a
    lot"): rip locally, let the router rebuild, and judge its output by look, not by
    keepQuality's default thresholds alone (they flag every long run's via pair as
    "2 new vias (needs 0)", which handed back 26 nets). Hand back only what is really
    bad, and fix placement for those first. Moved parts must not land on vias
    (optimizePlacement now refuses it; placementScan lists vias in pads).
  - A signal may **jump** under a two-pad part, straight between its pads (0.15 mm
    under an 0402 keeps 0.165 mm to each pad). The router does it too, on its
    `under-part-jumps` branch (`HUMAN_ROUTER` at a checkout of it).
- Under the screen the front (F.Cu) is bare: the panel sits on it. Assembly is double-sided
  since proto 1 (owner, 2026-10-22): in the chin the front carries the cell clips, the
  joystick, the radio's SWD pads and power parts that thin out the back (the USB buck and
  the power mux), under one pocket in the bezel's chin. The art lives on the back.

- **Routing defects: judge the copper, not its author** (owner, 2026-10-25: "whether something
  stays or not shouldn't depend on whether I did it, but on whether it's actually good").
  `scripts/routeDefects.py` lists only real defects, each tested on merit: a hop only when its
  own layer has a short way (grid search), a turn only in open copper, a GND track into the
  fill only when the pad does not need it (DRC decides), all of DRC at every severity, power
  necks, vias in pads, stubs. Calibrated on f5cf3e3 (the router's chin: finds the BAT_N neck
  and the pointless RADIO_RXD_MCU hop) and 7d25393 (the owner's: none of his deliberate hops).
  Fix what is mechanical yourself (stubs, DRC, silk), one change at a time under DRC counted
  by type, and hand the owner only what needs judgement; then give him a window to look.
  Never run the router's `gndRepair=join`.
- **Check widths, not only geometry.** The router once ran the cell's return (BAT_N)
  0.8 → 0.15 → 0.8 mm through the cell-sense resistor's pad; DRC and three review passes
  missed it (owner, 2026-10-25: "what the fuck"). `routeDefects.py` lists such "necks";
  read them after every routing pass.

## Traps

- KiCad `Flip(FLIP_DIRECTION_LEFT_RIGHT)` on these footprints mirrors **y**, not x.
  Bottom-side rotations are derived empirically and asserted by `checkFpc.py`.
- `src/parts/mcuAssign.json` can drift from `board/netlist.circuit.json`; `checkMcuAf.py`
  diffs them. Re-export the netlist after editing assignments.
- EasyEDA's footprint API 403s after bursts (`checkParts.py`, easyeda2kicad). Stale
  `land: null` cache entries then read "cannot verify": delete just those keys, rerun.
- pcbnew Python: one `LoadBoard` per process (a second one kills the first handle);
  the plotter ignores the hidden flag on board text (remove it in memory instead).
  After any `board.Remove()`, `GetDrawings()`/`Pads()` of other objects can come back
  broken: collect what you need first, remove last. A board saved next to a missing or
  default `.kicad_pro` gets default design rules: keep copies beside the real project. Load a
  board only as `wallet.kicad_pcb` beside its project: loading `other.kicad_pcb` (no
  project beside it) and saving it as `wallet.kicad_pcb` writes default rules over the
  real `wallet.kicad_pro` (2026-10-23: every DRC after that read 199 width/drill errors).
- `tsci export` reads every file under the entry's folder and dies on the vendored
  firmware's dangling symlinks: export with `scripts/exportNetlist.sh`.
- `SaveBoard` keeps the zone fills as they were: after adding copper by script, run
  `ZONE_FILLER(b).Fill(b.Zones())` before saving, and gate the saved file with a DRC
  *without* `--refill-zones` too (that flag refills in memory and hid a new BAT via
  sitting in a stale GND fill, which the Gerbers would have shorted; 2026-10-25).
- KiCad's DRC alone misses joins that hold only by track overlap; the router's tidy pass
  guards this, recheck after any track narrowing.
- Rotation offsets for JLC come only from JLC's placement preview, never from a guess.
- `partialRoute.py` / `routeGauge.py` drive humanizedKicadAutoRouter from
  `../../tools/humanRouter` (or `HUMAN_ROUTER=/path`). It is its own repo; commit
  router changes there. Run one board at a time (≈2 GB RAM each).

## Writing

- Name parts, not designators: "the SE050 secure element", "the 1 M airgap sense
  resistor `R_x`" — the designator after the name, when the reader must find it.
- Say "battery gauge", never "fuel gauge".
- A revision changelog is a diff of the actual schematic/BOM, not a paraphrase.
- Never call the board "fab ready"; report what the checks cover and what they don't
  (impedance, IR drop/thermal, enclosure fit, power-up behaviour are not covered).
- Commits: no GPG signing (`git commit --no-gpg-sign`); push to origin after committing.
