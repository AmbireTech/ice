Ambire Signer

A Safe-owner signing device. Its keys are generated inside a secure element and never leave it, so there is no seed phrase. It decodes every Safe transaction on its own screen. A physical switch cuts the radio and USB data, and the clear case shows the whole board.

What actually lost money, 2024–2026
$1.4BBybit, Feb 2025. Signers approved a Safe transaction their hardware wallets could not decode; it was a DELEGATECALL.
~$120MColdcard, Jul 2026. A build bug generated seeds from a weak random-number generator.
$72–93MLedger reseller implant, Sep–Oct 2026. An LTE implant was hidden under the screen; the tell was a visible wire.

None of these needed a lab. Each answer is a design choice: decode the Safe transaction on the device, generate keys inside a certified chip, and make implants visible.

Status

Proto 0 board is being routed by hand. It is 48 × 85 mm, 4 layers, 121 parts, all assembled by JLCPCB except the screen, camera and cell. The placement is hand-tuned, with related parts packed tight and decoupling caps against their pins. Keep-outs mark where the display tails, camera and artwork go. The wiring inside each block and the USB pair are pre-routed; the buses between blocks are being routed by hand. Every chip's wiring has been checked against its datasheet pin table, and an independent review's fixes are in.

  • Bench the OV2640 against a global-shutter SC031GS on an animated QR before committing.
  • The touch cable is long (56 mm). For production, a shorter cable or a single-flex panel.
  • The STM32U585 is thin at JLC (54 in stock), so production needs Global Sourcing. The SE050E2 has 594 at JLC and about 14,000 at DigiKey.
  • The colour print needs white solder mask, ENIG, and an order through EasyEDA Pro.
  • Firmware: Safe transaction decoding, ERC-7730 descriptors, an attested device certificate checked by the Ambire app.
