Ambire Signer

A Safe-owner signing device. Its keys are generated inside two secure elements from two different vendors and never leave them, so there is no seed phrase. It decodes every Safe transaction on its own screen. A physical switch cuts the radio and USB data, and the clear case shows the whole board.

What actually lost money, 2024–2026
$1.4BBybit, Feb 2025. Signers approved a Safe transaction their hardware wallets could not decode; it was a DELEGATECALL.
~$120MColdcard, Jul 2026. A build bug generated seeds from a weak random-number generator.
$72–93MLedger reseller implant, Sep–Oct 2026. An LTE implant was hidden under the screen; the tell was a visible wire.

None of these needed a lab. Each answer is a design choice: decode the Safe transaction on the device, generate keys inside a certified chip, and make implants visible.

The board, routed

Copper

  • Fully routed. KiCad's design-rule check reports 0 violations and 0 unconnected nets.
  • Four layers: parts and block wiring on the back, a solid ground layer beneath it, a 3.3 V plane, and the front for the buses between blocks.
  • The USB data pair to the main chip is routed as a differential pair.
  • Both outer faces are poured as ground and stitched to the solid ground layer with free vias. Parts reach ground through the pour rather than a via of their own, except where a short path to the plane matters: the buck-boost power stage, the charger's exposed pad, the radio module, the USB ESD clamp and the backlight charge pump.

First keyNXP SE050E2 secure elementsecp256k1 and P-256 keys generated inside, never exported. The PIN retry counter lives in the chip. Public docs, open firmware.
Second keyInfineon OPTIGA Trust M V3An independent P-256 key from a second vendor. The Safe counts the device as one owner that needs both signatures, so breaking one chip yields one key, not the device. JLC has none in stock, so early boards can leave it unfitted.

TransportsUSB HID · BLE · QRWebHID for the extension, BLE for iOS and Android, animated QR (ERC-4527) when airgapped.
Seed modePlanned firmware on the STM32U585For a seed-phrase wallet that does not rely on a second secure element: the PIN is stretched by about a second of encryption inside the main chip, under a hardware-unique key that software cannot read. Guessing PINs off the device first needs a physical attack on the chip. Not built yet; the speed gets measured at bring-up.
RecoveryThe Safe itselfThe device is a replaceable owner, so losing it means swapping an owner.

Status · 17 October 2026

Proto 0 board is fully routed. It is 48 × 85 mm, 4 layers, 99 parts, all assembled by JLCPCB except the screen, camera and cell. KiCad's design-rule check is clean, and every chip's wiring has been checked against its datasheet pin table. It has not been ordered: the items below are still open.

In progress

  • The battery connector is being turned to face inboard, so the mated plug stays inside the case. The renders on this page still show it facing the edge.

Open from the pre-order review

  • More ground vias at the buck-boost converter and under the charger's exposed pad.
  • Line width of the back silkscreen.
  • Labels for the programming pads, which have none yet.
  • Which way the airgap switch's lever points in each state, to be confirmed.
  • Part rotations, checked in JLC's assembly preview.

Later

  • Bench the OV2640 against a global-shutter SC031GS on an animated QR before committing.
  • The touch cable is long (56 mm). For production, a shorter cable or a single-flex panel.
  • The STM32U585 is thin at JLC (54 in stock), so production needs Global Sourcing. The SE050E2 has 594 at JLC and about 14,000 at DigiKey. The OPTIGA has none at JLC; finding it a source is a low priority.
  • The colour print needs white solder mask, ENIG, and an order through EasyEDA Pro.
  • Firmware: Safe transaction decoding, ERC-7730 descriptors, the two-key owner, the seed-mode PIN stretch, and an attested device certificate checked by the Ambire app.
